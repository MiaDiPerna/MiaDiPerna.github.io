SUMMARY
-------
The problem: A sports team sells tickets on several online platforms at once, and no single place shows the combined total. The operations manager can't get a clear, current count of tickets sold. That leads to overselling, empty seats, and missed payouts.

The solution: SeatSync, a phone app with five screens (Connect websites, Home, Game, Payouts, Invite staff). It shows one live total across every ticket website, takes sold seats off the other websites, sends a notification when a game reaches 10% of seats left, and flags any website payout that doesn't match its sales.

1. THE PROBLEM
======================================================================

A sports team sells tickets on several online platforms at once, and no single place shows the combined total. The operations manager can't get a clear, current count of tickets sold.

WHO HAS IT
----------
The operations manager of a professional sports team, who is responsible for ticket sales. The missing count causes problems at every stage of the season:
- Game week: deciding on promotions, staffing, and whether to release more seats.
- Game day: last-minute online sales come in fast on several platforms before doors open.
- After the game / end of month: matching each platform's payout to what it actually sold.
- All season: regular sales reporting to team leadership.

WHAT IT COSTS THEM
------------------
Mainly money, lost in three ways:
- Overselling: the platforms together sell more tickets than there are seats, and the team pays for refunds, upgrades, or comps.
- Empty seats: the team thinks a game is close to selling out, stops promoting it, and seats go unsold.
- Missed revenue: sales aren't reconciled, so money that platforms owe the team is missed or delayed.

TODO: Rough dollar figure per game or per season (research for evidence.md).

HOW THEY HANDLE IT TODAY
------------------------
They pull sales data from each online platform into spreadsheets and combine it by hand.

TODO: Names of the platforms (team website, Ticketmaster, resale sites, etc.).

WHY THAT FALLS SHORT
--------------------
Each platform reports separately, so there isn't always one clear total. Combining the numbers by hand is slow, and the result is out of date as soon as it's finished. That opens the door to overselling and missed payouts.

HOW THEY WOULD KNOW IT IS SOLVED
--------------------------------
- Speed: the manager can see the total ticket count across all platforms in under a minute, without any spreadsheets.
- Freshness: the count updates in real time (or within a few minutes), so no platform sells seats that have already sold elsewhere.
- Revenue: every platform's payout is matched to its sales, with nothing missing at the end of the month.

About the sources: most sources describing this problem are companies that sell ticketing software, so they have a reason to make the problem sound big. Each finding is labeled with its source type. Every claim below was checked against its source page on 2026-11-10.

DATA
----

1. Ticket sellers list on several platforms
- The NFL says that "rather than requiring season-ticket holders to list separately across different platforms to reach competing audiences," "eligible tickets can be listed once and distributed through multiple participating marketplaces." Ticketmaster currently has exclusive access to that resale inventory, and under the change "other authorized secondary marketplaces will gain access to season-ticket resale inventory from all 32 NFL teams." SeatGeek has reportedly agreed in principle to take part. News article. [1]

2. Reports from different systems disagree
- Box-office reports can conflict: "different reports can easily contain different data," and "time must be spent reconciling figures and working out which data is accurate." Promoted content written by a ticketing software CEO. [2]

3. Cost A: overselling
- Listing on several platforms splits your inventory, and "each one needs to be updated whenever a sale is made." Otherwise, "overselling becomes a real risk." Vendor blog. [3]
- Overselling "becomes a gate-day crisis and a refund program." Vendor blog. [4]
- A double booking can bring "refunds, card disputes, and a bad review." Venue software blog, not about sports. [5]

4. Cost B: empty seats
- Ticket Compare estimated the Kansas City Chiefs lost $2,164,542 per home game from "over 21,000 unfilled seats on average," at an average ticket price of "just a shade over $100." It analyzed "22 NFL teams that were unable to fill their stadiums," and "ten teams reported zero empty seats." Analysis by a ticket comparison company, method not published. [6]

5. Cost D: missed or mismatched payouts
- Payouts are based on net sales, which subtract ticketing fees and taxes from total revenue, and "refunds and chargebacks can also affect the total amount sent." Eventbrite "holds 20% of your net sales in reserve," paid out with the final payout. Platform help docs. [7]
- "A statement can show strong gross sales and a much smaller net payout," because of fees, refunds, chargebacks, and reserves. Vendor blog. [8]
- Payment "errors come from manual data entry, duplicate transactions, mismatched records across separate tools." Vendor blog. [9]

4. THE SOLUTION
======================================================================

Based on the interviews from 2026-11-10. The survey answers came in on Nov 12 (2 responses, see evidence.md). They didn't change this plan, because neither respondent has done the job, so their answers were guesses. Revisit the plan if someone who works in team ticketing or operations answers.

IN ONE SENTENCE
---------------
SeatSync is a phone app for the operations manager of a professional sports team. It connects every website the team sells tickets on, shows one live total, stops overselling, and checks that each website pays what it owes.

WHAT SOMEONE DOES WITH IT
-------------------------

First time only:
1. Open SeatSync.
2. Log in to each ticket website once to connect it.
3. The manager invites staff by email. Each staff member gets their own login and sees the same screens as the manager.

Checking sales:
1. Open SeatSync. Home shows each game with tickets sold and seats left across all websites.
2. Tap a game to see how many tickets each website sold.
3. Whenever a seat sells on one website, SeatSync removes it from the others.
4. When a game reaches 10% of seats left, SeatSync sends a phone notification.

Checking payouts:
1. Open Payouts.
2. Each website shows "Matches" or "Flagged."
3. Tap a flagged website to see what it sold, what it paid, and the difference.

THE SCREENS
-----------

1. Connect websites (first time only)
- Shows: the list of ticket websites.
- Tap a website: opens its login, which connects it to SeatSync.

2. Home
- Shows: each game, with tickets sold and seats left across all websites.
- Tap a game: opens the Game screen.
- Tap Payouts: opens the Payouts screen.
- Tap Invite staff: opens the Invite staff screen.

3. Game
- Shows: total sold and seats left, then how many tickets each website sold.
- Tap back: returns to Home.

4. Payouts
- Shows: each website's sales, its payout, and "Matches" or "Flagged."
- Tap a flagged website: shows what it sold, what it paid, and the difference.

5. Invite staff
- Shows: the staff who have been invited.
- Tap "Invite": type a staff member's email, and SeatSync sends them their own login.

There are only these five screens. Staff see the same screens as the manager.

WHAT IT DOES NOT DO
-------------------
SeatSync does only the four things in the one-sentence description. It does not:
- sell tickets
- set or change ticket prices
- count box office or walk-up sales (online websites only)
- scan tickets at the gate
- contact fans or do marketing
- make charts, reports, or predictions
- send any alert other than the 10% seats-left notification
- support more than one team
- replace the team's ticketing system

Anything not listed in "The screens" is not part of SeatSync.

WHERE THE INFORMATION COMES FROM
--------------------------------

What the manager types in (once): their login for each ticket website, and the email of each staff member to invite.

What the app gets from the websites: ticket sales, seats per game, and payouts.

What the app works out: the combined total, when a game hits 10% of seats left, and which payouts don't match their sales.

NOTES KEPT FROM EARLIER DRAFTS (NOT PART OF THE TEMPLATE)
---------------------------------------------------------
- Pricing: yearly subscription per team. TODO: Price per year.
- Why not an all-in-one system like Fever: no switching websites, cheaper, and a simple phone app built for the manager.
- Risk: SeatSync only works if ticket websites let outside apps read sales data. Automatic seat removal also needs permission to change listings.
- Risk: pro teams often have big ticketing contracts already. Ticketmaster currently has exclusive access to NFL season-ticket resale (see evidence.md), which could limit which websites SeatSync can connect to.
